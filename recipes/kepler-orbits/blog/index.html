01 / THE WORLD

Generate the orbits.

Seed 42 generates 256 planar two-body orbits with μ = 1: semimajor axis a ∈ [0.5, 4], eccentricity e ∈ [0.05, 0.3], and random orientations and phases. Solving E − e sin E = M gives exact position–velocity observations at uniform time intervals.

Larger a → longer period. Eccentricity changes the shape, not the period at fixed a.

Each orbit has 4,096 samples at Δt = 0.05. Whole orbits are split into 205 training and 51 validation trajectories; normalization uses training observations only.

The model sees [x, y, vx, vy]. Orbital parameters remain metadata. Training samples transitions with replacement: 10,000 batches × 256 pairs = 2.56 million sampled pairs.

02 / THE LEARNING RULE

Learn to predict.

The online encoder turns the current observation into 32 coordinates. Four predictors each read that state and predict eight coordinates of the next observation.

FOUR PREDICTORS4 × (32 → 8 → 8)one branch per factor

A target encoder defines the future representation. It receives no gradients, but follows the online encoder by EMA: θtarget ← 0.99 θtarget + 0.01 θonline. A learnable projection, initialized at identity, divides its output into four factors.

THE ACTUAL OPTIMIZATION OBJECTIVE

L = Lprediction + 0.10 LGram + 0.05 Lfactor activity + 0.02 Lencoder variance

PredictionMSE between predicted and target factor coordinates.

Soft Gram constraintEncourages orthogonality; does not enforce it exactly.

Two activity floorsPenalize target-factor and encoder coordinate standard deviations below 0.1.

Adam · learning rate 0.001 · batch 256 · 10,000 steps · CPU. Generation, training, and analysis took 46 seconds on skoltech_container, in a tmux session after mamba activate menv. The environment reuses the container’s existing numerical packages. Architecture, optimizer, and step budget are reconstruction choices; the paper does not specify its orbital training settings.

03 / THE READOUT

Extract the frequency.

Evaluate 32 fresh, in-range trajectories with seed 43, log-spaced axes, and 16,384 samples each. Encode and project observations, demean the coordinates, apply a Hann window, then take the strongest nonzero FFT peak with sub-bin interpolation.

Peaks are selected without consulting theory. Power is summed within each factor and across all factors for the aggregate. Frequency-bin spacing is 0.0012207; interpolation supplies no uncertainty interval.

04 / THE INTERPRETATION

What does it mean?

The representation preserves Kepler’s orbital rhythm. The matched zero-step encoder gives the same slope: this diagnostic does not demonstrate that training learned the law. The generator already supplies the period, and a pointwise encoder can retain it.

The implementation passed the recipe and core checks; remote checks verified all 10,000 steps and final artifacts. This is a reconstruction of the paper’s Figure 9, not an exact protocol reproduction or a physical-forecasting benchmark.

Prediction is a separate test.

Freeze the final EMA encoder and score all 208,845 adjacent transitions from 51 held-out orbits. The learned predictors beat persistence in this fixed latent space; that is evidence of useful one-step prediction. It does not establish a physical forecast, an autoregressive orbit, or a training benefit on the scaling slope.

Prediction error falls by 61.18% against EMA-target persistence. This comparison uses one checkpoint; no uncertainty across training seeds is available.

The projection is softly regularized: full rank, condition number 1.10166, maximum Gram-entry error 0.02444. It fails strict orthogonality tolerance 10⁻⁵, so exact orthogonal guarantees and factor independence are unsupported.

Inside the paper’s Figure 9, the authors select seed 0 by run-level median frequency error and report symplectic damping ρ = 1. Our Hann-FFT estimator and single seed do not reproduce that unspecified mode/damping procedure.
